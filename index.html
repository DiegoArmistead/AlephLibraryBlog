Personal library · reading that endures

The goal is not to read books. It is to interconnect them.

Aleph turns what you read—your highlights, your notes, your questions—into a living graph of ideas. Apple Books measures minutes. Aleph measures connections.

You read something that moves you. You highlight it. Six months later, the idea is still there—but you don't know where.

Your library shouldn't be a list of finished books. It should be everything you've thought, interwoven and at your fingertips.

Four core concepts, not a hundred features.

01

Active Harvesting

Highlight, annotate, draw by hand. Each idea remains linked to its book, its page, and the questions it raises. Nothing is lost.

02

A Graph, Not a List

Relate ideas across books; the axis is the author. Your reading becomes a network that grows with you, not a dead archive.

03

Provable Reading

Spaced repetition and staged progression that cannot be faked. Not just "marked as read": truly assimilated.

04

On-Device Intelligence

Apple Intelligence, on-device, suggests answers from your own library when you face a bottleneck. Private by design.

Every highlight is a node. Every relationship, an edge.

The metric of success isn't pages read. It's the density of connections—how much of what you read translates into capacity.
