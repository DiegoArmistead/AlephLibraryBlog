You read something that moves you. You highlight it. Six months later, the idea is still there—but you don't know where.

Your library shouldn't be a list of finished books. It should be everything you've ever thought—interwoven, alive, and at your fingertips.

Every highlight is a node. Every relationship, an edge.

The measure of success isn't pages read. It's the density of connections—how much of what you read turns into capacity you actually carry.

Four core ideas.
Not a hundred features.

Aleph does a few things with severe intention—each one in service of a single thesis: reading should compound.

01

Active Harvesting

Highlight, annotate, even draw by hand. Every idea stays tied to its book, its page, and the question it raised. Nothing dies in a margin.

02

A Graph, Not a List

Relate ideas across books, with the author as the axis. Your reading becomes a network that grows with you—not a graveyard of finished titles.

03

Provable Reading

Spaced repetition and staged progress you can't fake. Not "marked as read"—genuinely assimilated, and verifiable over time.

04

On-Device Intelligence

Apple Intelligence, on device, answers from your own library when you hit a wall. Private by design—nothing leaves your phone.

Read. Assimilate. Create.

01 — READ

Let the reading flow

The reader stays calm and frictionless. Analysis is always optional and never in your way. First you read—everything else waits.

02 — ASSIMILATE

Turn ink into models

Distill each book to its core: the author's position, the central mechanism, its failure modes, the one operational principle you'll keep.

03 — CREATE

Join the conversation

Your ideas become outputs—essays, themes, positions—and, one day, a conversation with friends who read closely too.

Built to feel native. Down to the last pixel.

Aleph is Apple-first, and Apple-only. It borrows the calm of the system it lives in—system fonts, native gestures, on-device intelligence—then quietly boosts it for the one thing it's built for: deep reading.
